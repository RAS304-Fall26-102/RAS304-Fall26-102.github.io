# Brainstorming Process

## Who participated

The ideation effort was completed by the full project team working together on the modular robotic leg design. Team members contributed ideas across multiple technical areas, including mechanical architecture, actuation, sensing, control, and end-effector design. The process was collaborative and based on group discussion rather than individual isolated design work.

## How the team met

The team met regularly during the concept development phase to discuss the project direction and generate design ideas. Meetings were used to review the project goals, define design constraints, and evaluate new concepts as they emerged. The team also used these sessions to compare alternative approaches and refine promising features before finalizing the concepts.

## How ideas were collected

Ideas were collected through structured team brainstorming sessions. Each member contributed design options for the leg architecture, actuation strategy, sensing approach, and interaction with the ground. Features were recorded as individual concepts, then discussed as a group to clarify purpose, feasibility, and relevance to the project. This ensured that the design space remained broad before concepts were narrowed down.

## Which project requirements were used

The brainstorming and ranking process was guided by the project requirements developed for the robotic leg system. The team used the needs and constraints from the project specification, including:

- functional mobility and locomotion capability
- feasible mechanical design for a six-legged robot
- realistic actuation and power requirements
- sensing and control suitability
- manufacturability and modularity
- safety and robustness
- compatibility with the overall robot platform and use case

These requirements acted as the selection criteria for deciding which features were worth pursuing in more detail.

## Additional resources used during ideation

During ideation, the team used several supporting resources to expand and evaluate possible design directions. These included:

- prior project research on walking robots and legged locomotion
- benchmarking of existing robotic leg and hexapod systems
- literature and examples from modular robotics and actuator design
- design discussion informed by course project requirements and team goals
- sketches, concept diagrams, and comparison notes generated during meetings

These resources helped the team ground the brainstorming process in practical engineering considerations while still encouraging creative exploration.

## How the features were grouped

After the initial list of ideas was generated, similar concepts were grouped into categories based on their function. Features were organized by design area, such as:

- joint architecture and kinematics
- rotary and linear actuation
- feedback and sensing
- ground interaction and foot design
- modularity and maintainability
- structure and manufacturing
- safety and control

Grouping the features allowed the team to identify common design themes and avoid evaluating isolated ideas without context. This also made it easier to combine complementary ideas into complete product concepts.

## How rankings were assigned

The team assigned rankings by evaluating each feature against the project requirements and overall system value. Features were judged based on criteria such as:

- how directly they supported the robot’s movement and stability
- whether they improved performance or controllability
- their manufacturability and integration difficulty
- safety and reliability
- modularity and future flexibility
- expected usefulness for the selected concept direction

Features that best matched the project goals and seemed most feasible were ranked highest. The highest-ranked ideas were then recombined into the three product concepts that were developed and compared later in the design process.
